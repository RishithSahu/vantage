# 13. Limitations, open gaps and roadmap

Read this before relying on any number in this folder. These are the things we know are weak, in plain words, with what would close each.

## 1. The big gaps

| Gap | Why it matters | What would close it |
|---|---|---|
| **No real vehicle has ever been attached.** Every car is a recording or a simulation replayed as its real wire traffic. The SocketCAN collector has run only on virtual interfaces and with a stand-in socket. | The case study is about a vehicle. Encoder and decoder were written by the same team (the decoders are checked against independent libraries, but traffic is generated by our own emitter). | One real capture through the platform; a real CAN controller in listen-only mode; a drive in a real car |
| **Understanding is graded on questions we wrote.** 84.3 % exact on unseen-wording sets, 91.5 % on older sets; no outside tester; the one outside-authored set (500 questions) has no gold. | A model can score 95 % on its authors' questions and fail a stranger. | A new set written by someone outside the team, double-annotated, scored as frame and as "what the person is shown" |
| **Confident mistakes.** 58 % of wrong frames had confidence of at least 0.8. | Confidence cannot catch most errors; the refusal line at 0.50 catches only about 17 % of wrong readings. | Label repair and training on the model's own confident errors ([10](10_research.md)) |
| **Latency is on average, not for every question.** Pi 4 median about 1.4 s, but 22 to 27 % of questions are above 2 s (long prompts: 17.7 % of all; database-bound: 4.4 %). | The case study says under 2 s for common queries. | Shorter prompts (changes answers), a faster CPU (Pi 5, A55-class), cheaper per-token kernels, tier answers for the heavy routes |
| **No user study.** Nobody but the owner and a few testers has used it. | Value for drivers and engineers is argued, not measured. | A study with people outside the team (the scaffold exists; never run with real participants) |
| **Safety isolation is a software boundary on one host.** Measured on a Pi 4 with a real-time task; no hypervisor, no partition, no ISO 26262 work product. | A certified vehicle needs more than an argument. | A partition or hypervisor design, a hardware data diode, a HARA done with a manufacturer |

## 2. Platform gaps

* Run only on Windows, Linux on a Raspberry Pi 4, and Android Automotive (Cuttlefish) as a data source. **Not run:** QNX, a Yocto image, a hypervisor guest, a Pi 5, an AWS Graviton instance, a vehicle HPC, a GPU.
* "Scalability to a vehicle HPC" is measured on a 12-thread laptop standing in.
* The Android display app, a Binder client for the Vehicle HAL, a reader for the QNX CAN driver and a Yocto recipe were not written. QNX and Android logs have a tested forwarder that never ran on a real device.
* A container file was written on 21 Oct (`deploy/docker/Dockerfile`), never built, and is no longer in the tree.
* Cuttlefish: the stock fake Vehicle HAL has fixed read-only speed and gear, so the Android car does not move. A moving car needs a simulator behind the HAL (the owner mentioned CARLA integration being done elsewhere).
* TPM-backed keys were never run on hardware (the Pi 4 has no TPM).

## 3. Model and understanding gaps

* Weakest areas (audit of 25 Oct): why (80 %), advice (81 %), off-topic (82 %), unsupported (84 %), "unlisted" things the car does not have (69 %), throttle position (33 %), figurative language (about 40 % on a very small sample), Hinglish (about 90 %), long preambles (73 %).
* Neighbouring concepts the model confuses: throttle against accelerator position, regen power against regen energy, fuel rate against Wh per km, battery overheat warning against battery temperature, now against health.
* **Follow-ups** are the weakest learned skill: 87 % right on a held-out set of 167 complete questions asked after another answer; "how was yesterday" typed after a battery question may inherit the battery.
* **Petrol and diesel** were learned later and have a smaller training base; "battery power" asked of a diesel is answered with the engine's power (the one known substitution in the answerability audit). Diesel sets score slightly lower (87.9 %).
* The model cannot say correlation or "than usual" (frame 2 added these; the sets are small).
* Training data and the primary evaluation sets were written by the same author (an AI assistant working with the owner); the training banks were written after the sets were frozen and overlap-filtered, but this reduces, not removes, leakage. No local teacher model was available; no cloud model is used at run time.
* Executors for *topic* meanings (health, advice, charging time and cost, service) are older keyword route handlers called with a machine-written canonical request; they still contain keyword logic inside.
* Interim rules listed as debt (`docs/TRAINING_QUEUE.md` section 10): the typed "which wheel?" ask, the trip-pick parse, the parked-loss selection by a "last night" period, `fuel_remap`, the unsafe and grounding floors, follow-up completion, investigation routing.

## 4. Data and analytics gaps

* The generated worlds are smoother than a real car's data; real buses are noisier and bigger.
* Analyses outside the 32 investigation shapes fall back to a single-frame answer.
* Explanations: the cause engine is validated on planted causes (87.8 % first answer). Alignment drift against added drag is weak (1 of 4 first answer). It does not claim causal inference beyond what the signals support, and answers "nothing changed enough" when appropriate.
* Heavy routes (vehicle health, EV compare and detect-change) still read millions of minute rows into Python (2 to 19 s on the Pi before the 26 Oct fixes; about 3 s for battery-health trend on a laptop). Moving them to tiers would change their data-quality wording.
* Days with no trips give "highest speed 0 km/h" (true, plain wording). Open requests: "4 trips in that one day", "longest trip about 4 days back", a code-class filter (P/B/C/U) for fault codes.
* Statistics on a real fleet were never run: the fleet hub was tested with 16 simulated cars.

## 5. Security and privacy gaps

* A compromise of the running assistant with the key loaded can read the clear database in RAM.
* No third-party penetration test; the camera path and the speech recogniser had no independent security review.
* Per-driver profiles inside the driver role, retention-based deletion of personal data on request beyond the driver's erase button, and a hardware key store are not done.
* Dataset licences (VIF-AEGIS, HCRL, comma2k19, Open-Meteo) still to be verified before redistribution; a `LICENSE.txt` is still to be chosen.

## 6. Housekeeping on record

* The 26 Oct speed work and everything after the v1.3.4 tag are **not committed** and not in any installer; a 1.3.5 build and tag are still to be made. The v1.3.4 installer lacks the Pi fixes.
* The Pi's password was shared in a chat and a no-passphrase key was installed: rotate and remove.
* `VantageCli version` once printed a stale constant (1.2.3) (24 Oct note; recheck).
* Disk: results caches (`results/_cache` prefix states, about 9.8 GB), old installers (3.5 GB in `deploy/windows/dist`), backups and temporary folders can be pruned when space is needed.

## 7. Roadmap (in the order we would do it)

**Next, no training needed**
1. Measure frame to final answer on the audit's 385 errors (how many change what the person is shown).
2. Label repair: cleanlab pass over training and test rows, a second annotator on the failures, merge or define near-synonym pairs, score near-synonyms as equivalent.
3. Per-question latency profile on the Pi (prompt tokens, passes, data time) for the slowest question shapes; trim hint lines under the accuracy gate (baseline 90.0 % overall, 84.3 % on unseen sets).
4. Commit the speed work and cut a 1.3.5 build and tag; re-run the release gate; re-measure the three car types on the Pi.
5. A fresh outside-authored, double-annotated evaluation set.
6. Test on a Pi 5 and an A55-class board if available.

**Next, with the owner's approval to train**
7. Preference optimisation on mined confident mistakes plus hard-pair contrastive and "unlisted" examples, on the repaired labels, gated against the old sets.
8. A low-confidence nearest-example second opinion (no training, added time only on about 8 % of questions) if the owner accepts any added time there.
9. Classification heads for act, concept and operator if a decoder change is acceptable (saves 3 to 5 decode passes).
10. Drop a few layers and re-tune, or single-token labels, for speed.

**Beyond the case study's demo**
11. A real vehicle capture and a real listen-only CAN controller.
12. A partition or hypervisor design with a hardware diode; QNX and Android Automotive hosts; a Yocto recipe.
13. A user study with drivers, service engineers, fleet operators and validation engineers.
