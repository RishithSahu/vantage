This is the one current report. The earlier working log, with measurements from older revisions that are now superseded, is archived in `docs/history/VANTAGE_STATUS_working_log_2026-10-06_07.md` and must not be quoted. Every accuracy number below was regenerated on **one source revision** (hash `532a35ed31282e76`, 132 Python files; `results/final_manifest.json` also records model, database and question-file hashes and runtime versions). After that run only the performance script's configuration switch and the acceptance freeze/run scripts changed (none of them affects accuracy results); the current source hash is `228beb7cbc3dcc6e` (`results/final_manifest_current.json`), and the freeze script records the hash at freeze time.

## 1. What VANTAGE is, in brief, and the honest summary

An on-device vehicle assistant: a deterministic rule parser and typed query IR, a whitelisted read-only SQL planner and executor, evidence contracts, an answer verifier, an **interpretation contract** (provenance + support + coverage checks), a deterministic **candidate generator** and **clarification-first policy**, optional frozen-model candidate selection (LFM2.5-350M Q4_K_M, 229 MB) or the earlier Qwen-0.5B + LoRA whole-query model, schema overlays for other vehicles, and ingestion decoders for CAN/SOME-IP/VHAL/logs/NMEA (replayed, not live).

What the measurements support, in plain terms:
* The **engine is the strong part**: windows, aggregates, counts, rankings, retention behaviour, paging and the explanation arithmetic are checked against independent SQL/numpy; schema changes (different columns, units, scales, missing and new signals) work end to end.
* **Language understanding is the weak part and generalises poorly.** The rules alone answer 77 of 120 development questions correctly, but only 137 of 308 (44%) on the older final set (regression data), where 26% of their answers are wrong. The development numbers are optimistic (rules were improved while reading dev failures).
* **The clarification-first policy is a risk/coverage choice, not a free win**: it answers more (84 vs 69 dev answers; 176 vs 143 final) and recovers more questions after one clarification (94 vs 77 dev; 179 vs 137 final) but its wrong answers rise (12 vs 8 dev; 56 vs 38 final).
* **The small model's contribution is not demonstrated.** The frozen LFM2.5-350M used for candidate selection is worse than no model (section 5); the earlier fine-tuned Qwen-LoRA gives modestly more correct direct answers, at p95 latency above 2 s.
* **The deterministic configuration is very light**: no model, 105 MB process peak, common-query p95 32 ms on the laptop (section 8). That configuration answers the system-level requirements but **does not use an SLM**, which the case study asks for; whether a model earns its place is the open question.
* **No ARM measurement exists.** All timings are x86 laptop cores (2 pinned). QEMU is deferred at the owner's request.
* The sealed **acceptance set is an unreviewed draft** and has never been run on any system.

## 5. Bounded model-role experiment (pre-registered; one run per configuration)
Variant: a deterministic generator proposes readings; the frozen LFM2.5-350M only chooses an index; a chosen reading is answered only if it passes support + coverage, otherwise offered as a clarification.
* **Candidate availability** (is the intended query among the candidates?): 84 of 108 answerable dev questions (77.8%), above the pre-registered 70% floor.
* **First run (earlier revision, baseline = rules + policy without the generator):** all pre-registered criteria met (wrong answers 12 vs 13; correct + clarifications containing the intended reading 90 vs 85, margin +5 against the required +4; model-path p95 1.1 s; context fits).
* **Final revision, same criteria, baseline = rules + generator + policy (no model):** wrong answers 11 vs 12 (met), but correct + clarifications containing the intended reading **90 vs 94 (fails)**; on the old final set 167 vs 179. The deterministic generator that the policy now uses makes the frozen-model selection unnecessary, and the selector lowers directly correct answers (-8 dev, -16 final, p = 0.0004).
* **Transfer criterion (new signal described only by label and synonyms, 10 questions):** the right reading was answered or offered on 9 of 10, but 7 of the 10 were answered by the rules directly (the overlay rebuilds the vocabulary); the model-attributable part is 1-2 questions (`results/transfer_select.json`).
* **Verdict: the frozen small model has no demonstrated role in this system.** Stopping rule applied: the selector is not tuned further.
* **The earlier LoRA** gives more directly correct answers than the no-model policy (+5 dev, +7 final; not significant in this sample) at equal wrong-answer counts, but it is a fine-tuned model, ignores conversation history through its interface, and its model path exceeds 2 s at p95 on the laptop (dev p95 2.4 s). Recommendation: retain it as the evaluated baseline and as an optional fallback; do not claim the system's accuracy comes from a frozen small model. A model that is trained to choose among candidates, or to fill only unresolved fields, is the untested next step and was not attempted (no new training).

## 7. Interpretation, clarification, schema transfer, executor
* **Interpretation contract v2** (`vgpt/interpretation.py`): provenance (question / parts / cue / prior turn / default / vague / removed / ambiguous / ungrounded) for operation, targets and wheel position, statistic, both periods, thresholds (comparator, number, unit and referent recognised separately), groups and their order, selection, event type/selector/window, units; support and coverage checks; disclosures state the actual period. 14 of 138 gold queries (dev + diagnostic) are flagged on the final code; each flag is attributable (gold periods that differ from documented defaults, genuine ambiguity, lexicon gaps). 25 contract tests plus 7 policy / dialogue tests.
* **Clarification-first policy** (`vgpt/policy.py`, `vgpt/candidates.py`, `vgpt/paraphrase.py`): answer / clarify with concrete readings / refuse; commands are refused (read-only); a question the rules recognise as being about recorded data is asked about, not handed to a fuzzy route (e.g. "how often was it colder than 5 degrees last month" offers outside, battery and motor temperature, and "1" answers the first). Clarification quality on dev: no-model policy 9 of 21 contain the intended reading; with the selector 13 of 28.
* **Schema transfer and missing capability** (`bench/schema_fixtures.py`, `tests/test_schema_transfer.py`, 10 end-to-end tests on edited storage): missing front-left pressure (never replaced by another wheel; the overall figure names which wheels exist), aggregate-only pressure, different stored units and scales (offset supported), registered-but-empty signal, intermittent signal (day coverage stated), motor vs steering torque, a brand-new signal supporting all generic operations, an opaque identifier (`CH_0417`) known only through its description. **Not covered:** a vehicle whose buses (not storage) differ; renaming identifiers of signals the legacy engines use.
* **Executor / retention:** exact interval enforced before aggregation (partial days, boundary samples; one midnight-crossing test is skipped because no trip crosses midnight in the fixtures); coverage is judged against days with recorded trips or charges, needs half of a day's active minutes to count as covered, follows derived quantities to their signals, inspects both comparison periods, keys numbers per signal, reports unknown coverage (no ledger) as unknown. Event lists can be retrieved in full by paging (`evs=pageK`, "next"); the concatenated pages equal the SQL list.
* **Explanations:** the decomposition is distance-weighted so the explained quantity equals the headline (total energy / total distance). Against counterfactual twins (13 roof-box pairs, effect-size strata, none dropped): the unrecorded cause stays in the unexplained part by a median 0.70 (min 0.30), recorded factors absorb a median 0.30 (more than 0.5 in 2 pairs), absolute error median 6.7 Wh/km (max 20.3). In 6 two-cause families (roof box unrecorded + gradual tyre leak recorded) the recorded tyre factor takes 2-6 Wh/km credit although its true effect is about 0 (it moves with the period); the answer now says when a factor moved together with the period, and the roof effect left unexplained is a median 0.52. Change points are labelled exploratory (serial correlation). **Associated, not causal.**

Selector cold start 1.2 s (model load 0.45 s + prompt-state build 0.75 s for its 82-token prompt; the earlier whole-schema planner prompt was 3,696 tokens, so the prompt-state build that stalled under QEMU does not apply to this configuration, but that is not measured on ARM); warm start 0.44 s. The ingest daemon uses about 23 MB and processes 502 messages/s at real-time pace with lag under 1 s on the same two cores; with ingestion running, selector common p95 was 1.05 s (`results/perf_final_concurrent.json`). 90-day history (1,189 trips, 16 MB, 3x the fixtures): the slowest queries fell from 1.5-1.9 s to 0.2 s after two fixes (a loop-invariant standard deviation in the sensor-quality check; a vectorised permutation test that matches the original statistic to 1e-12): full-history average 85 ms, change detection 202 ms, explanation 209-252 ms. 300 extra signals: overlay 44 ms, parse 48 ms, candidate generation 47 ms, execution 12-33 ms; a whole-schema prompt would be about 27,000 characters (does not fit a 4,608-token window), which the candidate configurations avoid. Attribution is not the latency bottleneck (250 ms on 90 days). **Every measured path is under 2 s on the laptop; the Qwen-LoRA path reaches 2.4 s at p95; ARM timings will be several times slower and are an open requirement.**

## 11. Open items and limitations
1. **ARM hardware or QEMU latency and memory: not measured** (deferred by the owner). `python vm/driver.py smoke` runs the guest benchmark; results must be labelled emulation.
2. **Acceptance set unreviewed and never run.** Review package: `docs/acceptance_review/README.md`; freeze and run-once scripts: `bench/freeze_acceptance.py` (needs an explicit `--system` choice), `bench/run_acceptance.py` (neither has been executed).
3. Rules and conversation sets were tuned on failures and are regression data; the old 308-question set influenced the interpretation vocabulary. Only the sealed set can measure generalisation, and one author wrote the vocabulary and every question set.
4. The clarification-first policy trades errors for coverage: on the old final set it gives 56 wrong answers among 176 (32%), against 38 of 144 (26%) for the rules alone, and 3 false actions of 21 refusal questions against 1. Choosing the operating point is an engineering decision, not something the case study supplies.
5. Clarification quality is low on unfamiliar wording (54 of 83 clarifications on the final set offered no intended reading). The user is asked, not misled, but the interaction cost is real.
6. **The SLM requirement is not met by evidence**: no configuration in which a small model improves accuracy at a latency that meets 2 s has been demonstrated.
7. Event lists, paging and retention were verified on generated 28- and 90-day histories only; protocol decoders run on replayed traffic; no real vehicle, CARLA or Raspberry Pi run exists.
8. Opaque identifiers are supported for new signals registered by overlay; renaming identifiers of signals the older engines hard-code is not.
